Welcome to part 2 of our "Meet MAST" series. If you've missed the others, check them out here (link).

This interview is with David Rodriguez, one of our staff scientists. David is the lead of Blue team, which is responsible for the main MAST database and our astroquery API (among other things!)

## What brought you to MAST?
While I trained as an astronomer, after my first postdoc I was a bit disillusioned with the prospects of going into academia. I started looking into data science careers since that was getting lots of attention, but felt daunted by the industry expectations. I was looking for a job that balanced data science and software development in an astronomy setting.

## Describe how you spend a typical day-to-day at MAST.
I wear a lot of hats at MAST, being a scientist, developer, and product owner all at the same time. I juggle a lot of tasks but my favorite project is when I work on the Common Archive Observation Model, our back-end data model that captures metadata for all our missions. It may not be glamorous, but it's the backbone of a large part of what makes MAST so useful.

## What is your workspace like? Do you work from home, or the office? Desk or couch?
I work in a hybrid scenario, coming into the office about half of the time. I like my office setup with large windows, a standing desk, a variety of nerdy book posters, and plenty of friendly folks around to chat with.

## What are your hobbies?
It may not be too surprising to learn that I am a bit of a nerd. I enjoy science fiction and fantasy and consume a lot of that in various ways. I play video games, watch movies and TV series, and read books which I later review in my personal blog. I even run the occasional tabletop role-playing game with my friends.

## What excited you most about the future at MAST?
I'm excited to see how we make all of MAST's various datasets work together. Behind the scenes, a lot of our data is isolated from each other, but scientists easily associate products together—think images, catalogs, target properties, etc. I'm hoping we can build a way for our users to link together all the data they are interested in.

## If you were a space telescope, which one would you be?
I would be GALEX. I would have a wide field of view, not focusing on any single target in particular, but always on the lookout for eccentric oddities.
